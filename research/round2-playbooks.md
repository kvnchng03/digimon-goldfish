## TS Mervamon (Homeros control)

Source keys: [OM] digimonmeta OvermasterP "3 Gods 3 Protections 3 Memory Merva Megazoo" (2026-08-28, read in full, cached as `guides/merva_megazoo.txt`); [DC] DigiCarding profile + goldfish JSnmWFvks5E; [KE-T] KnTElixar Titans vs Merva 0iT08uRH52Q; [KE-R] KnTElixar vs Red Hybrid NTJDzVUjaMU; [DD] DDOUGHY Merva vs DATS Rosemon -jFZg3bprFk; [OS] Osaka top 16 fqG5g4TfBfk; [GD-P] Peoria Glowing Dawn profile qjWEEf2UKxM; [GD-M] Madrid Glowing Dawn profile vWJCKCJVDy0.
Caveat: no readable source is a TS pilot explaining the Glowing Dawn matchup specifically.

**Mulligan**
- Keep any hand with a Lv3; 12 Lv3 searchers because "those level sixes are expensive" (STATED [DC]).

**Turn plan**
- Early: land an ADAMAS Tamer during the climb via BT24 Aegiomon; take own security early with Aegiomon / Aegiochusmon: Holy because Wide Plasment costs +1 per security card (STATED [OM][DC]).
- Full combo (STATED [OM]): Mervamon plays Homeros + a Lv3 searcher and gives -4000 DP per [TS] card until the end of the opponent's turn; at end of turn Homeros replays Mervamon's effect into a Lv5 (Sirenmon); the Tamer then uses Jupitermon // Wide Plasment (delete lowest DP, Recover +1, Arts Digivolve, play a [TS] card at -5: Bacchusmon for 2, which plays Sirenmon).
- Truncate the combo to fit the hand; short lines and small passes are "a serious strength" (STATED [OM]).
- Stopping at Lv6: Minervamon first, then Mervamon, for an extra Homeros (STATED [OM]).
- Plan B when Minervamon / Mervamon get removed or don't land: Ceresmon / Bacchusmon suspended board (STATED [DC]).

**Resources and memory**
- Typical early pass about 7 (Wide Plasment needs a pass at 5 plus 1-2 to extend); later, with Homeros memory and low security, choke the opponent to about 3 (STATED [OM]).
- Kanan Yuki lets them pass at 3 and still pay +2 for an option (STATED [DC]).
- Don't play Homeros every turn: opponents pass enough memory to suspend it; keep about two on board and hold one for the big turn (STATED [DC]).
- Deck-out is real in long games; Coronamon is in the list partly because it doesn't draw from the deck (STATED [DC]).

**When ahead**
- Only attack when safe; at "the point of no return" commit everything (STATED [DC]).
- Finishers: Dianamon unsuspends an attacker (can take 5 security against an empty board), Dan Yuki & Kanan Yuki extra attack, Mervamon's Alliance (STATED [DC]).
- Avoid time draws by drilling the deck (STATED [DC]).
- Plays around: Bacchusmon's mandatory deletion gets baited; Jupitermon has no protection once its sources are trashed (STATED [DC]); Crimson Blaze in security stops Mervamon's plays (STATED [KE-R]).

**When behind or disrupted**
- After a board wipe: Coronamon trashes Minervamon from hand to draw 2, then Mervamon Assembly replays pieces from the trash; replaying from the trash is why it wins long games (OBSERVED/STATED [DC]).
- Defensive layers: Sirenmon and Jupitermon "doesn't leave" (back to security), Mervamon/Minervamon grant Blocker + Reboot, Sirenmon/Dianamon inherited redirects, Aegiochusmon: Holy shields Mervamon from -DP, Minervamon's On Deletion deters attacks (STATED [OM][DC]).
- What works against it: keep Minervamon suspended instead of deleting it (stays out of the trash, Assembly harder) (OBSERVED [KE-T]); suspend/no-unsuspend locks beat Reboot and Rosemon Burst Mode bottom-decks Mervamon (OBSERVED loss [DD]); games drag when the pilot doesn't find Homeros (OBSERVED [KE-T]).

**Versus Glowing Dawn**
- DigiLab: TS Mervamon 21-4-4 vs Glowing Dawn (4 ties in 29, consistent with time draws).
- Glowing Dawn pilots (older formats): against a Blocker deck "you kind of have to tunnel in on the OTK" or the Blocker "ruin[s] your day"; Reboot decks can "completely shut down" Glowing Dawn (STATED [GD-P]); decks that force the long game deny the OTK; BT15 Armalize "suspends blockers" (STATED [GD-M]).
- INFERRED why ~84%: every Iliad body has Blocker + Reboot so blockers are always up on our turn; redirects and leave-protection (which adds to their security) undo a 5-check push; -DP lasting through our turn and Wide Plasment's option-card deletion both get around Atratusmon's immunity to Digimon effects.
- INFERRED Glowing Dawn outs: kill before Mervamon lands, suspend the blockers (Armalizamon), or play for time.

**Common misplays (STATED [DC] unless marked)**
- "Caveman mode" card spamming; feeding Homeros into a 5-memory pass; slow play into time draws; over-searching late into deck-out; letting Bacchusmon be baited; Wide Plasment while security is high; burning turns digging Minervamon for Assembly; building around Venusmon; passing extra memory into Minervamon (OBSERVED [KE-T]).

## Toho Braves

Source keys: [BR] Brisbane 2nd profile, pilot Caleb, July 2026 (EX12, pre-BT26) H40YtjjjJbE; [SPS] Sao Paulo top 8 Yuri vs Glowing Dawn, Portuguese 7JFJMCXLEA0; [RM] same match re-commentated olBMZIKhl5g; [RG] the Glowing Dawn pilot's interview TR9dZjK9XH0; [MD] Madrid Glowing Dawn profile vWJCKCJVDy0; [DB] Digi-Bros on Yuri's list tEhS-rspKPA; [SG] Singapore final Ky_clVcx92s; [RC] Rotherham final MDqMIDDu1Jo; [TCGP] tcgplayer "Best Decks for the EX12 Format"; [JJ] jumbojank.wordpress.com 2026-07-15 "Rushing the Combo"; [DL] digilab /deck/toho-braves.
Cached: `research/transcripts/gp-tb/`.
Auto-caption names normalised ("Gossa" = Gasamon, "Karkri" = Karakurumon).

**Mulligan / opening**
- Gasamon plus Genshi Continent face-up in security means "you will always be able to make it to a level six if your opponent passes you one" (STATED [BR]).
- Turn 1 Analog Youth plus a rookie in breeding is a trap: passed to 1 memory, you can't play next turn without Shishimamon; hence 3 Analog not 4 (STATED [BR]).
- Explicit mulligan rules: no data.
- Possible banlist "hit on Analog" mentioned by Sao Paulo commentators: check Analog Youth's legality.

**Turn plan and Lv6 choice**
- Turn 1: Island for 3, or Analog Youth plus a set Sanmyojin Arrival (OBSERVED [SPS][SG]).
- Kaguyamon is the default "turn two, push up a level six": four bodies, all Blockers with Retaliation; "you do die next turn" without an answer (STATED [BR]); "go-to first call... fastest way to get your board durable" (STATED [RC]).
- Ryugumon: almost never off Arrival, but essential into Atratusmon-with-Barrier (Glowing Dawn) and Galacticmon (STATED [BR]).
- Amaterasumon: removal, recovery, disrupts the opponent's trash; best card to play off Arrival (STATED [BR]).
- Weakness: its strongest plays need setup, which fast decks exploit (STATED [TCGP]).

**Resources**
- Restrict memory: Yuri chose a line passing Glowing Dawn 3 instead of 4-5 (OBSERVED [SPS]); "I don't want to give them too much memory" (STATED [DB]).
- Digivolve in breeding is "super important": no memory-generating Tamers to guarantee the next turn (STATED [BR]).
- Analog Youth gains memory after Execute deletions to keep the turn (STATED [JJ]).
- Trash for Susanoomon: usually enough names after the first Execute stack; hold Kokeshimon to delete own Digimon for one more name; Lv5s are the key names; Kaguyamon is weak unless the trash can supply all three bodies (STATED [BR]).

**When ahead**
- Cast Susanoomon every turn: recover, trash their security (STATED [BR]).
- Opponent passes 6-7 late: Susanoomon, pass them to 1, Execute, re-use via Analog Youth: about four checks and the game (STATED [BR]).
- At 0 security: Genshi Island recovers 1, Susanoomon, Arrival into Amaterasumon, Karakurumon: recovers about 3 (STATED [BR]).
- Holding Amaterasumon back baits removal onto the evading Ryugumon (OBSERVED [DB]).
- Ryugumon attacks and unsuspends: "virtually two checks" (OBSERVED [SPS]).
- Plays around: an option in security stopping Shishimamon; Iron Slash on the pushed stack (STATED [BR]).

**When behind**
- Island's [Security] plays a Lv5-or-lower from hand: a Shellmon (stun, Blocker) off a security hit; this froze the Glowing Dawn player's Habakirimon (STATED [BR], OBSERVED [SPS]).
- Arrival plays a Lv6 straight from hand if they can't climb; Arrival into Kaguyamon in response to removal gives the board Retaliation (STATED [BR]).
- Karakurumon stuns Tamers: TB's only way to touch Tamers (STATED [BR]).
- Deck-out / time: one game went to the last card; otherwise no data.

**Versus Glowing Dawn**
- DigiLab: TB vs Glowing Dawn 58.7% (88-62-14).
- "If you see Ryugu that matchup's pretty favored" (STATED [BR]).
- How Yuri beat the Glowing Dawn player: Ryugumon (evade survives while its effects still fire), and its trigger when Glowing Dawn plays or digivolves a Digimon punished them; Glowing Dawn lost game 2 playing into it (STATED [RG]).
- Game 1, which Glowing Dawn won: Atratusmon immune to TB effects; Glowing Dawn de-digivolved Ryugumon so Arrival couldn't trigger; Habakirimon trashed security to clear the board; Glowing Dawn won by "stealing the turn", dodging two checks, and turned the extra memory into lethal (OBSERVED [SPS]).
- Game 3, which TB won: early Kaguyamon with four Retaliation blockers, Shellmon locked a Glowing Dawn Tamer, Kunlun; Glowing Dawn bottom-decked Kaguyamon, Arrival brought another, "nothing changed"; removing Kaguyamon re-triggers Karakurumon and Kaguyamon, which "basically guarantees you take lethal back" (OBSERVED [SPS]).
- Habakirimon's bottom-deck is Glowing Dawn's best tool: denies Susanoomon pieces and avoids Retaliation (STATED [RG]).
- Madrid Glowing Dawn plan: clear all 5 security in one turn, because early chip feeds Susanoomon's materials; pump Atratusmon's DP (STATED [MD]).
- INFERRED: TB's key turn is the one after its first push; it keeps Arrival set and a blocker wall so Glowing Dawn can't steal the turn and OTK.
- What TB targets first (Tamers or the breeding Lv4): no data.

**Misplays**
- Shishimamon into a security option; not seeing Ryugumon against Atratusmon or Galacticmon; a trash too thin for Kaguyamon or Susanoomon; passing big memory ("you cannot give this deck copious amounts of memory") (STATED [BR]).
- Letting Glowing Dawn steal the turn while it has memory (OBSERVED [SPS]).

## Glowing Dawn (our deck)

Several of the strongest sources are BT25 era (June to August): Peoria, Cygnus, TCGplayer, Nima's Dusseldorf store regional, V-Tamer.
BT26-era: DigiCalise, GAO LCQ commentary, Sao Paulo games, Quang-Minh's list, Romulo's profile.
Researcher notes: `research/transcripts/gp-gd/notes.md`.
Source keys: [Peoria] qjWEEf2UKxM (Hoang Zero, top 16, BT25); [Cygnus] k3Bm2N4dHYg; [DigiCalise] MLlgdNbhFjc (BT26); [Nima] CoEe9A1S5mU (Gere Gaming); [GAO] u0z_9HckvRM (vs TS Box); [V-Tamer] DhHn8jxncWY (vs Jupitermon); [SC] VJmNkVlg3Y0 (vs Jupitermon); [Romulo] TR9dZjK9XH0 (Sao Paulo top 8); [Madrid] vWJCKCJVDy0; [SPtop8] olBMZIKhl5g; [Brisbane-TB] H40YtjjjJbE; [Barcelona] mZ4GiLpJzEs; [KnT] qiM2Bz92Vd0 (vs DATS); [Sydney] uNZ3ufPQFPI; [TCGP] tcgplayer "How To Build A Glowing Dawn Deck"; [CR] cardsrealm BT25 deck tech; [QM] digilab /decklist/116166.

**Mulligan / opening**
- Start every game with a Lv3 and a Tamer (STATED [TCGP]); "open with a Tamer and use your Rookies to find the pieces" (STATED [CR]).
- A Tamer later than turn 1-2 hasn't banked enough fuel to kill; would cut a searcher rookie for a second Reina & Makoto to find a Tamer faster and add a security turn-stealer (STATED [Peoria]).
- Always want ST23 Gekkomon to raise: its When Moving loads a Tamer (STATED [Nima]).
- Both losses in [GAO] and [SC] had no Tamer until turn 3-4; a no-Tamer game is "a wrap" in Bo1 (OBSERVED).
- INFERRED: mulligan any hand with no Tamer and no way to find one (e-Pulse, Gekkomon, Liollmon).

**Turn-2 aggression vs a later OTK**
- "You just can't chip": chipping into Tamer security is how you lose; go for the kill or sit back (STATED [Peoria]).
- Most common kill was from 5 security, ending over half their games by turn 3: swing BT25 Murasamemon (own top security as the cost), Final Judgment for +1 Security Attack, Kekkomon into Habakirimon (its burn hits the opponent because security is tied), unsuspend, finish; needs a Tamer and 2/3/4 memory from BT25 Murasamemon in the back / a Lv4 / Gekkomon (STATED [Peoria]).
- Contradiction: vs Jupitermon chip them to 2-4 security and race (STATED [Nima]).
- BT26 is slower and wider than BT25: 4 Atratusmon and 2 Habakirimon, immunity over OTK (STATED [DigiCalise]).
- Habakirimon combo into option-heavy security (its protection survives removal options); Atratusmon combo into Time Stranger decks, which remove with Digimon effects (STATED [Cygnus]).
- Took a line with spare memory to play around a security Tamer instead of the exact-memory line, and hit one (OBSERVED [Peoria]).

**Resources**
- Don't spend fuel early (STATED [CR]); 3-4 fuel to kill; BT25 Murasamemon can pay with security instead of fuel; only 1 Chiropmon because it costs a fuel (STATED [Peoria]).
- Always count needed fuel and know what is under each Tamer (STATED [Nima]).
- A memory setter that always restarts at 3 lets the opponent plan around it; +1 Tamers force them to choke lower, so Tomoro cut to 2 (STATED [DigiCalise] citing East); 2 Tomoro also for option discounts (STATED [Romulo]).
- Glowing Dawn option copies: Peoria 2 (late turns still kill); Madrid 0 (delays Tamer setup); Romulo would drop to 0-1; Quang-Minh 0.

**When ahead**
- Leave 1-2 spare memory so the kill survives a security Tamer flip; Three Musketeers' removal options key off play cost and DUAL cards have none (STATED [Peoria]).
- Best answer to Kaguyamon: bottom-deck it with Eclipse Impact, then digivolve to battle the blockers (OBSERVED [SPtop8]).
- ST23 Murasamemon's -5000 clears blockers (STATED [Romulo]).

**When behind**
- BT25 Armalizamon is the recovery card: off e-Pulse, suspends a blocker, makes Final Judgment cost 0-1; won a game when passed 6: Tamer, e-Pulse into Armalizamon, Final Judgment, Habakirimon burn, slam a Lv5 (STATED/OBSERVED [Peoria]).
